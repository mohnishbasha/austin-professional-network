# Austin Bhet Event Playbook

A reusable guide for planning an indoor, in-person Austin Bhet professional networking gathering. Adapt it to each event. **None of the examples here describe a confirmed event.**

---

## 1. Concept
- **What:** an indoor, professional, in-person gathering for job seekers, working professionals, students, and mentors in the Greater Austin area.
- **Why:** to build authentic relationships, share career experiences, learn practical skills, and find ways to support one another.
- **For whom:** everyone, not only job seekers. Each person who attends should get something out of it.

## 2. Planning timeline (relative to event day **E**)

| When | Task | Owner (role) |
|---|---|---|
| E − 8 weeks | Form an organizing team, set goals, and choose a theme | Lead organizer |
| E − 7 weeks | Shortlist venues and confirm the venue's requirements (security, ticketing, A/V) | Venue lead |
| E − 6 weeks | Identify sponsors for refreshments or space, and agree on what can be acknowledged publicly | Sponsor lead |
| E − 5 weeks | Publish the registration page (e.g. Luma) **only after date and venue are confirmed**, and post it to the LinkedIn group | Comms lead |
| E − 4 weeks | Invite presenters and panelists, and send them speaker guidance (§5) | Program lead |
| E − 2 weeks | Set the RSVP cut-off, finalize the program and timing, and collect slides | Program lead |
| E − 1 week | Send a reminder with logistics and confirm volunteers, refreshments, and A/V | Comms + logistics |
| E | Run the event (§4) | Everyone |
| E + 1–3 days | Send thanks, a short recap, and a feedback survey, and post next steps in the group | Comms lead |
| E + 2 weeks | Hold a retro and update this playbook | Organizing team |

## 3. Sample agenda (configurable duration)

Set `TOTAL` to the event length. Each block is a **share of the time** rather than fixed minutes, so the agenda scales. The example column assumes `TOTAL = 150 min`.

| # | Block | Share | Example @150 min | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Arrival & mingling | 10% | 15 min | Name tags; an icebreaker prompt on each table |
| 1 | Welcome & community introduction | 4% | 6 min | Purpose, guidelines, photo policy |
| 2 | Brief attendee introductions | 15% | 22 min | Timebox each person (e.g. 30–60 s). Split into groups if the room is large |
| 3 | Professional sharing: what you do today, or what you want to do | (part of block 2) | (part of block 2) | Combine with intros, or run in small groups |
| 4 | Short practical presentation | 12% | 18 min | Resumes, interviews, job search, or networking |
| 5 | Panel or lessons learned | 17% | 26 min | 3–4 professionals, moderated Q&A |
| 6 | Structured networking | 20% | 30 min | Rotations or interest tables. LinkedIn connections are optional and mutual |
| 7 | Informal conversation & refreshments | 17% | 25 min | Can run alongside block 6 |
| 8 | Closing remarks & next steps | 5% | 8 min | Thank volunteers and sponsors (with permission); explain how to stay involved |

Quick calculation: `block minutes ≈ TOTAL × share`, rounded so the blocks add up to `TOTAL` (the example rounds the halves to keep 150 minutes). Adjust the shares to suit the program. For example, a career-paths format might give block 4/5 a larger share for many short talks.

### Format variations
- **Career-paths edition:** many short talks, each covering one field: typical paths, education and certifications, where the jobs are, and resources.
- **Hiring announcements:** a short timeboxed slot (≈2 min each) where hiring managers describe open roles. No personal data is collected.
- **Speed networking:** short timed rotations. Plan the mechanics in advance so people meet more people.

## 4. Running the room
- **Mix the room.** Use icebreakers, assigned tables, or rotations so attendees don't cluster by existing friend groups or split along gender or seniority lines.
- **Timekeeper.** One volunteer owns the clock and gives speakers a polite signal.
- **MC.** One person introduces each block and keeps the energy up.
- **Accessibility.** Provide seating, a microphone if the room is large, and a clear path to restrooms.
- **Photo policy.** Announce it at the start, and give people a simple way to opt out.

## 5. Speaker and panel guidance
Send this to presenters:
- Time allotted, and whether slides are welcome (one or two slides of bullet points is plenty).
- **Keep it educational.** Don't advertise your own company or business.
- Suggested outline for a career-path talk: the field, typical paths, education and certifications, common employers, locations, and further resources.
- Ask whether the organizers may name them publicly before and after the event.

## 6. Venue considerations
- Indoor, suitable for conversation, introductions, and a short presentation.
- Tables and chairs, restrooms, and a projector or screen with laptop connection. Microphone and speakers are a bonus.
- Capacity matched to expected RSVPs, with some buffer.
- Any security check-in or ticketing the venue requires.
- Parking or transit access, and an accessible entrance.
- Cost: aim for free or low-cost space so the event stays affordable.
- **Don't announce a venue until it is confirmed in writing.**

## 7. Sponsor considerations
- What's needed: refreshments, venue space, A/V, printed name tags.
- Agree in writing on what the sponsor provides and **how (or whether) they're acknowledged**.
- No sponsor logos on the website or materials without explicit permission.
- Sponsors receive no attendee contact data.
- If sponsorship falls short, consider a small, clearly stated cover charge on the registration page so costs are shared openly.
- Track expenses privately among organizers. Don't publish who paid what.

## 8. Volunteer roles

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Lead organizer | Overall plan, decisions, team coordination |
| Venue lead | Booking, access, A/V, setup and cleanup plan |
| Program lead / MC | Speakers, panel, timing, introductions |
| Comms lead | Registration page, LinkedIn group posts, reminders, recap |
| Sponsor lead | Sponsor outreach, agreements, acknowledgement permissions |
| Logistics / refreshments | Food and drinks, name tags, signage |
| Greeters | Welcome people and help newcomers find someone to talk to |
| Timekeeper | Keeps every block on schedule |

## 9. Follow-up
- Thank attendees, speakers, volunteers, and (with permission) sponsors.
- Send a short feedback survey. Ask what worked, what to change, and topics for next time.
- Post a brief recap in the LinkedIn group. Name people only with their consent.
- If organizers approve a public recap, add it to `src/data/events.json` (see `docs/CONTENT_MAINTENANCE.md`).
- Keep attendee data private, use it only to run the event, and delete it when no longer needed.

## 10. Lessons from past gatherings (anonymized)
Organizer notes and attendee feedback from earlier gatherings suggested:
- Plan **icebreakers** that help everyone mix, so the room doesn't divide into separate groups.
- Speed networking was well intended. Plan its mechanics so people make the most connections in the time available.
- Make a **formal sponsorship ask** early, or use a small cover charge, to keep gatherings sustainable.
- Give attendees a clear way to volunteer to host future events.
